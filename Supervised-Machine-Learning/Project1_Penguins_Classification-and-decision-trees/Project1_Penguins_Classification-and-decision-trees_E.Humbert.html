Figure 3 : Commentary on the decision tree model¶

1. Root and leaf labels¶

The root node of the decision tree is labeled Adelie Penguin.
Since Adelie penguins generally have larger measurements than the other species, the entire sample is initially grouped under this label.

Investigating the leaves, we observe that only Adelie and Gentoo penguins are explicitly represented.
Chinstrap penguins, while present in the data, do not appear distinctly in the tree structure.
This suggests that Chinstrap samples are absorbed into other classes without clear separation.

The distribution is coherent in terms of sample counts:
the total value at the root node is divided between the two leaves.

2. Model behavior and potential underfitting¶

The decision tree splits the data based on features such as Culmen Length and Culmen Depth.
However, because max_depth = 1, the model only makes a single split, leading to a very simplistic structure.

As a result:

  • The model fails to create distinct paths for all penguin species.
  • Chinstrap penguins are not well isolated and likely get classified as either Adelie or Gentoo, depending on their measurements.
  • This indicates that the model is underfitting the data, unable to capture the complexity needed to distinguish all species.

3. Practical implications¶

In a real-world prediction scenario, this model would not be effective for correctly identifying all penguin types.
Without deeper splits or additional distinguishing features, Chinstrap penguins are at a high risk of being misclassified or ignored.

To improve the model:

  • Increase the tree depth to allow more complex decision boundaries.
  • Incorporate additional features that better differentiate Chinstrap penguins from the other species.

Key insights¶

The model, with its shallow structure, underfits the data and overlooks Chinstrap penguins.
For better performance, the tree needs deeper splits and potentially richer feature information to achieve finer-grained classification.

Figure 4 : New sample probability to belong to a penguin class¶

If this new sample existed, the predicted probabilities of belonging to each penguin class would be:

  • 96.5% for Adelie Penguins,
  • 0.9% for Chinstrap Penguins,
  • 2.6% for Gentoo Penguins.

Explanation based on the decision tree¶

The high probability for the Adelie Penguin can be explained by the structure of the previously created decision tree:

  • The root node and one of the leaf nodes are labeled as Adelie Penguin.
  • Additionally, the new sample has a culmen length of 35 mm, while the maximum culmen length in the dataset is around 42 mm.
    • Since 35 mm is relatively short compared to the maximum, the model is more likely to classify the sample as Adelie.

Behavior within the leaf nodes¶

Once the sample reaches a leaf:

  • Only the Gini impurity value remains to guide the prediction.
  • Since the culmen length is under 42.35 mm, culmen length is no longer used at this point.
  • Observations:
    • One leaf has a Gini value of 0.067, indicating low impurity (almost pure).
    • The other leaf has a Gini value of 0.52, indicating higher impurity.

Note:
A Gini impurity of 0.067 still reflects a certain level of disorder, but not maximum — maximum impurity for binary classification is 0.5 (not 0.067).
A pure leaf (containing only one class) would have a Gini impurity of 0.

Influence of class distribution¶

  • By examining the value parameter in each leaf, we observe that each leaf node contains samples from all three species.
  • However, Adelie Penguins dominate, which explains why the model assigns such a high probability to Adelie and low probabilities to Chinstrap and Gentoo.

How to improve the model¶

Adding more depth to the decision tree would likely:

  • Allow the model to create more specific splits.
  • Improve classification probabilities for Chinstrap and Gentoo penguins.
  • Potentially reduce the overwhelming bias toward Adelie Penguins.

Increasing depth would help the model better capture the nuances between the species based on culmen measurements and other features.
